## 1 Course Description

The course provides foundational knowledge of enterprise information systems, with a particular focus on Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) and Customer Relationship Management (CRM) systems. It examines the role of enterprise information systems in organizations through the lens of the opportunities and challenges they create, and through their use and impact on organizational operations.

### Guiding questions

- What information systems support business operations and strategy?
- How do ERP and CRM systems support business operations and strategy?
- What kinds of innovation do enterprise systems enable, and what challenges do they raise?
- How do enterprise systems integrate into, and reshape, organizational processes?

### 1.1 Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to:

1. understand what kinds of information systems support organizations, from operational and transactional systems to managerial and strategic platforms, and situate enterprise-wide systems within this broader organizational landscape;
2. understand the fundamentals of enterprise information systems, with a particular focus on ERP and CRM systems;
3. analyze the role of enterprise information systems in organizational operations, identifying the opportunities and challenges they present;
4. evaluate the use of enterprise information systems in various business contexts and their impact on organizational efficiency and competitiveness;
5. describe and explain how enterprise information systems integrate into organizational operations and affect business processes;
6. apply learned concepts and theories to practical business situations related to enterprise information systems.

### 1.2 Competencies and Skills

The learning objectives are aligned with IS2020, the joint ACM/AIS competency model for undergraduate programs in Information Systems. Skill levels use Bloom's Cognitive Taxonomy (1 Remember through 6 Create).

| LO | IS2020 Competency Area | IS2020 Competency | Skill Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations of Information Systems | Classify the components, elements, operations, and impact of IS (incl. common types of IS such as Transaction Processing Systems and Enterprise Systems) | 2 Understand |
| 2 | Foundations of Information Systems | Critique and recommend Enterprise Systems for a given business problem and processes | 3 Apply |
| 3 | Foundations of Information Systems | Analyze a business case and critique appropriate IS solutions to common business problems, based on the different components, elements, types, and levels of IS | 3 Apply |
| 4 | IS Management and Strategy | Ensure operational efficiency and effectiveness in service delivery of organizational information | 5 Evaluate |
| 5 | Business Process Management | Explain the characteristics of a process and the different perspectives of a process model | 2 Understand |
| 6 | Business Process Management | Use process improvement methods and implement TO-BE processes by eliminating bottlenecks, enhancing, and innovating the AS-IS process | 3 Apply |

Objectives 1-3 draw on the Foundations competency realm. Objective 4 draws on IS Management and Strategy, and objectives 5-6 on Business Process Management, both part of the Organizational Domain competency realm.

## 10 Assignment Overview: Individual and Group Work

Assignments combine theoretical understanding, practical system competence, collaborative skills, and reflective practice, aligned with the flipped classroom pedagogy. Individual assignments are the theoretical exercises, hands-on practical exercises, and a learning diary. The group assignment is video production. Together with the final exam and active on-site participation, these form the complete assessment framework.

### 10.1 Individual Assignments

#### 10.1.1 Theoretical Exercises (2 exercises, total 3 hours)

Short, low-stakes exercises that connect course concepts to real-world observations and digital demonstrations. They require physical presence or visual evidence, which limits the value of AI assistance even where technically allowed.

**Exercise 1: Campus Process Walkthrough.**
Visit a physical service point on the JYU campus or a retail service in the city (e.g. IT Helpdesk, Student Services, Library, Super Market, Sport equipment shop, 2nd Hand Shop). Observe a staff-student or staff-customer interaction and produce a UML sequence diagram of the process, then identify what people, organizations and systems are at play. Also share the place where the observation took place. In the last week of the course, revisit the exercise, reflecting on how you would produce the diagram again after learning across all the course sessions.
AI policy: Allowed, but discouraged. The diagram must reflect genuine direct on-site observation. AI tools may be used only for diagram formatting, not for inventing or completing process steps.

**Exercise 2: Dual YouTube Video Analysis.**
Watch two pre-selected short video demos curated by the teacher (one ERP system, one CRM system). Take timestamped screenshots (clearly showing the YouTube progress bar and video title) and write a brief comparison of the data each system manages.
AI policy: Allowed, but discouraged. AI cannot generate timestamped screenshots of specific URLs, so direct viewing remains necessary.

#### 10.1.2 Hands-on Practical Exercises (3 exercises, total 18 hours)

Individual exercises that build from basic user interaction to programmatic control of the open-source ERP/CRM suite Dolibarr. Each requires interacting with the live system and submitting a short report with screenshots and code/output.

- **Exercise 1: UI Navigation.** Core data-entry tasks entirely through the Dolibarr web interface: creating products, customer accounts, sales orders, and generating a PDF invoice. Deliverable: report with screenshots and a brief reflection on the user experience. See an ERP/CRM system as a regular user.
- **Exercise 2: SQL Queries.** Connect to the Dolibarr database with a SQL client and write queries to extract business intelligence (listing customers, checking stock levels, joining order and customer tables, calculating sales totals). Deliverable: SQL code, result screenshots, and a short business interpretation of each result. AI policy: Encouraged, provided you can explain what each query does. See an ERP/CRM system as a single database across one organization.
- **Exercise 3: REST API Interaction.** Use Postman, curl, or a Python script to authenticate and perform CRUD operations against the Dolibarr REST API (GET, POST, PUT). Deliverable: HTTP requests, JSON responses, and a brief explanation of the authentication mechanism and data structures. AI policy: Encouraged, provided the final submission is understood and can be explained. See an ERP/CRM system as a web service that connects organizations over the Internet.

#### 10.1.3 Learning Diary

Throughout the teaching period, keep a personal reflective diary in Markdown format. Students are encouraged to use the university's GitLab (https://gitlab.jyu.fi/), which has an online editor and Markdown render. Entries reflect on weekly themes, guest lectures, hands-on experiences, and the connections between theory and practice. The diary is edited as the course progresses and counts for 12% of the final grade.

### 10.2 Group Assignment

#### 10.2.1 Video Production Assignment (1 exercise, approx. 12.5 hours per student)

Groups of 3-4 students.

- **Task:** Select one peer-reviewed article from the course's journal or conference reading list. Break down the article's research problem, methodology, key findings, and practical implications for organisations.
- **Production:** A 5-7 minute video that explains these elements clearly to a non-specialist audience. It must include at least 2 minutes of on-campus footage (e.g. a walk-and-talk, a whiteboard explanation in a study room, or a library research segment) and a visual summary (slides, whiteboard, or graphics). At the end, a credits visualization acknowledges who contributed (appear in camera, video recorded by, video edited by, slides by, graphics by, voice over by, etc).
- **Deliverables:** Final video file or unlisted URL (e.g. YouTube) and a short script/storyboard (1 page).
- **AI policy:** Allowed with disclosure. AI may be used to brainstorm the script, generate slide titles, or edit text or video. The core analysis and on-campus footage must be produced by the group. All software tools used for the video and all AI usage must be clearly disclosed in the video itself.

Detailed instructions, rubrics, and submission deadlines are provided in separate handouts released progressively on the course learning platform. Begin group work early and attend the hands-on sessions for support from the teaching team.

On-site participation accounts for 15% of the final grade. **All students must take and pass the exam, and all students must do the group work.** Students do not need to pass or do all the individual exercises or attend all lectures to pass the course, but they need to accumulate **50 points out of 100** across the assessment components. Work on all group and individual assignments can start from the 2nd week of the course.
